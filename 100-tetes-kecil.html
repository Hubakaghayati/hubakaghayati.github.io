A Small Message of Humanity

100 Tetes Kecil

Small Acts. Meaningful Messages.

100 Tetes Kecil is a simple expression of hope — that 100 people and institutions around the world can become part of a stream of peace through humanitarian songs, each carrying one small drop of kindness.

We want to become part of those drops by sending songs about peace, humanity, hope, and life to public figures and institutions connected with peace, humanity, interfaith dialogue, leadership, culture, education, and the environment.

They are people and organizations whose voices, actions, and influence may help carry this message of humanity further — from one heart to another.

These songs are not meant to be grand solutions.
They are simply small messages from one human being to another.

And perhaps, one drop may seem very small.
But when many drops come together, they may become something greater.

One human being. One song. One message.
100 drops for humanity.

Why I Send This Song

I am not a leader. I only have a song.

I am not a world leader, diplomat, religious figure, or someone with the power to stop a war.

I am simply an independent songwriter from Indonesia.

When I see suffering, hatred, violence, and division, I sometimes ask myself: “What can I do?”

I cannot change the decisions of governments. I cannot stop a war by myself. But I can still make a song.

Perhaps one small drop cannot extinguish a great fire.
But I still choose to carry water.

There is a well-known wisdom story about a tiny creature carrying water toward a great fire.

It is told that when Prophet Ibrahim, peace be upon him, was about to be thrown into the fire by King Nimrod, a small ant saw the enormous flames.

The ant knew that its body was tiny. It knew that the amount of water it could carry was almost nothing.

Yet it went to fetch water and carried it, little by little, toward the fire.

A crow saw the ant and asked:

“Why are you carrying that water? Do you really think such a small amount of water can put out such a great fire?”

The ant replied:

“I know this water will not be enough to put out the fire.”

But it continued carrying the water.

Not because it believed it could extinguish the fire. But because it wanted to choose which side its action stood on.

The message is simple.

I know that one song link from an ordinary person may not stop a war.

I know that one song may not change the decisions of those in power.

I know that the person receiving this message may even never open the link.

But I still send it because I want to say:

“I cannot stop that great fire.

But I do not want to simply stand by and watch it burn.

This is the small drop of water I can carry.”

I am not doing this because I believe I can save the world.

I simply do not want to remain silent when the world needs more voices calling for peace.

One human being.
One song.
One message.
One small drop of water.

That is why I am sending this song to people from different countries, religions, professions, and backgrounds.

Not to teach them.

Not to ask anything from them.

Not to represent a particular religion, nation, or political side.

Only as a small invitation to remember that, whatever our faith and wherever we come from,

we are all human beings, and we all deserve peace.

“But I still choose to carry water.”

The Drops

One message at a time.

The 100 recipients are kept private while this journey is being prepared. Only messages that have actually been sent will be shared here.

The journey has begun.

Each time a song and message are sent to one of the people or institutions chosen for this journey, that drop may be added here.

The purpose is simple: not to announce a list of intentions, but to share the small acts that have actually been carried out — one message, one recipient, one drop at a time.

When a drop is sent, it becomes part of the story.
One Human Being to Another

Maybe it is only a drop.

Maybe some people will never open the message. Maybe some will listen and simply move on.

That is okay.

I only want to make sure that, while the world is still struggling with hatred and war, I choose to carry a little water.

From Indonesia, with hope for humanity.
